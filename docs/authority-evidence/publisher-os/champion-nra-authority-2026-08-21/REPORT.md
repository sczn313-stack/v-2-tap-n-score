# Champion NRA Target Authority Investigation

Scope: authority investigation and evidence preservation only; no registration, activation, application implementation, production change, commit, push, or deployment.

## Founder result

The NRA publicly defines three governed target specifications relevant to the four Champion commercial products: **B-16**, **B-2**, and **B-3**. Champion's `GB-*` strings are commercial designations; they are not NRA target designations found in the current NRA Precision Pistol Rulebook or Marksmanship Qualification Program guide.

Champion product images resolve SKU 40749 `GB-16` to a target face printed `B-16`, and SKU 40751 `GB-3` to a target face printed `B-3`. Champion's literal `B-16` SKU 40722 is the same governed B-16 specification in a different commercial pack. The public evidence strongly maps SKU 40750 `GB-2` to NRA B-2, but only a low-resolution first-party catalog thumbnail was available; that mapping therefore remains fail-closed at Grade B pending a legible face/seal confirmation.

## What the three NRA specifications mean

### NRA B-16 — 25-yard slow-fire pistol target

- Official name: 25-yard slow fire, Standard American type, target B-16.
- Champion published sheet size: 10.5 × 12 inches. The current NRA rulebook governs the scoring-ring geometry but does not publish the cut-sheet size as an NRA specification.
- Black: rings 7 through 10.
- Ring diameters: X 0.67 in; 10 1.51 in; 9 2.60 in; 8 3.82 in; 7 5.32 in; 6 7.22 in; 5 9.66 in.
- Values: X is a 10 used for tie-breaking; the other rings score their printed value. A hit whose leaded edge touches the outside edge of a higher-value ring receives the higher value. Outside ring 5 is a miss.
- Current competition uses include the 25-yard NRA Short Course slow-fire stage: 10 shots in one 10-minute string. The Marksman League course also uses B-16 for its 25-yard slow-fire stage.
- Current MQP use: B-16 slow fire paired with B-8 timed/rapid at 25 yards. Slow fire is 10 rounds in 10 minutes or less. The MQP is governed by a separate qualification profile; the B-16 paper alone does not establish a qualification claim.

### NRA B-2 — 50-foot slow-fire pistol target

- Official name: 50-foot slow fire; reduction of the 50-yard Standard American target to 50 feet; target B-2.
- Champion published sheet size: 10.5 × 12 inches.
- Black: rings 7 through 10.
- Ring diameters: 10 0.90 in; 9 1.54 in; 8 2.23 in; 7 3.07 in; 6 4.16 in; 5 5.56 in; 4 7.33 in.
- The current specification lists **no X ring**. The center is the 10-ring.
- Values and edge scoring follow the same Rule 14 procedure; outside ring 4 is a miss.
- Current competition use: NRA Gallery Course at 50 feet, slow fire, 10 shots in one 10-minute string. The rulebook also records 20-shot 50-foot slow-fire national-record courses.
- Current MQP use: B-2 slow paired with B-3 timed/rapid at 50 feet in the Conventional Bullseye Pistol course.

### NRA B-3 — 50-foot timed/rapid-fire pistol target

- Official name: 50-foot timed or rapid fire, target B-3.
- Champion published sheet size: 10.5 × 12 inches.
- Black: rings 9 and 10.
- Ring diameters: X 0.90 in; 10 1.80 in; 9 3.06 in; 8 4.46 in; 7 6.14 in; 6 8.32 in.
- Values: X is a 10 used for tie-breaking; outside ring 6 is a miss; Rule 14 edge scoring applies.
- Current competition use: NRA Gallery Course at 50 feet, timed fire as two 5-shot strings at 20 seconds each, then rapid fire as two 5-shot strings at 10 seconds each. The rulebook also records separate 20-shot timed and rapid 50-foot courses.
- Current MQP uses the same timed and rapid string structure with B-2/B-3 at 50 feet.

### Firearm, position, and course ownership

The target does not independently establish every course condition. Under the current MQP Conventional Bullseye Pistol course, any rimfire or centerfire semi-automatic pistol or revolver may be used; metallic, telescopic, and electronic sights are permitted unless they project an image on the target. Shooting is standing, one-handed, except that the first two ratings permit benchrest or two-handed standing. Current Precision Pistol competition has its own equipment categories and match program. SCZN3 must bind an exact course/profile version rather than infer conditions from the target SKU.

For MQP qualification, the 30-shot aggregate is 10 slow, 10 timed, and 10 rapid shots in one session. Current published aggregate thresholds are 125/300 twice (Pro-Marksman), 175/300 twice (Marksman), 210/300 twice (Sharpshooter), 250/300 twice (Expert in the aggregate table), and 269/300 ten times or 258 in two NRA-sanctioned competitions (Distinguished Expert). The guide also contains a stage-skill table with separate repetitions; implementations must select one exact governed course, not blend the tables.

- 40722 — B-16 — 25 yd Pistol Slow Fire — 10.5 × 12 — 100 pack
- 40750 — GB-2 — 50 ft Slow Fire (Train & Qualify) — 10.5 × 12 — 12 pack

The current Champion/Primos catalog feed observed 2026-08-20 exposes 40722 and 40751 as available variants. It does not expose 40749 or 40750. Therefore 40722 and 40751 are current commercial records; 40749 and 40750 are historical publisher records unless newer first-party evidence is obtained.

The current catalog image assigned to the 40722 variant actually depicts the 12-pack barcode for 40749 and prints `B-16` on the face. This is useful evidence that `GB-16` is Champion's commercial/package identity for B-16, but it is also a current catalog asset mismatch and must not be used to claim that the pictured pack quantity describes SKU 40722. The 40751 image prints `B-3` and carries an NRA Official Competition seal.

## Champion ↔ NRA relationship

These are two separate conclusions:

1. **Target-specification authority:** yes. NRA-authored current sources establish B-16, B-2, and B-3 meanings independent of Champion.
2. **Champion's present formal status:** not established. The NRA's current licensed-manufacturer page says licenses renew regularly and lists American Target Co., National Target Co., Speedwell Targets, and U.S. Target Inc.; Champion is not listed.

Historical first-party Champion target images carry the NRA Official Competition seal, and current NRA Rule 4.1 says official competition targets and the seal are restricted to NRA-licensed manufacturers using NRA masters. That is strong evidence of a historical authorized printing relationship. It does not prove that Champion currently holds a license. Champion's current commerce page calls the products NRA-approved, but that publisher claim cannot override the current NRA directory. Retailer statements that Champion is licensed are corroboration only.

**Fail-closed consequence:** SCZN3 may use public NRA authority for practice/simulation scoring once the product-to-spec mapping is admitted. It must not label a current Champion sheet eligible for NRA-sanctioned competition, or issue an official NRA qualification/recognition claim, without current printer/license and program-administration authority.

## Cross-publisher reconciliation

The evidence supports the model **one NRA-governed target specification → multiple licensed printers' commercial implementations**.

- NRA's current list names American Target, National Target, Speedwell, and U.S. Target as licensed printers.
- American Target currently lists official B-16, B-2, and B-3 products.
- National Target currently lists NRA B-16, B-2, and B-3 products and NRA royalty metadata.
- Speedwell currently lists official B-16, B-2, and B-3 products.
- NRA's own U.S. Target manufacturer sheet lists B-16, B-2, and B-3.

Among the six requested existing P-OS publishers:

- Baker, Splatterburst, Highwild, and Birchwood Casey: no exact B-16/B-2/B-3 equivalent was established in the preserved catalog evidence.
- EZ Aim / Allen SKU 15777-50 includes `B-2` in its product name, but it is a 23 × 35 Max Michel Course of Fire target. It is **not** the NRA 50-foot B-2 specification.
- GunFun sells colored products named B-3 (for example SKUs 32010–32080) at a published 9.5 × 12 size, but the preserved publisher descriptions establish colored circle targets—not NRA approval, exact NRA ring geometry, or sanctioned equivalence. They remain candidates, not equivalents.

Sheet-size listings also vary by printer/site (9.5 × 12 versus 10.5 × 12). The NRA rulebook publishes ring diameters, not the public cut-sheet specification. SCZN3 should preserve each commercial product's actual published dimensions and bind scoring through the governed ring geometry; it must not infer equivalence from sheet size alone.

## SCZN3 architecture result

No new capability family is indicated.

- **Smart Evidence Capture** can record the known publisher product and shooter evidence without a richer claim.
- Existing **precision/open scoring** can consume the NRA ring-value authority and UGO circle geometry.
- Existing **Cue/Drill stage/event primitives** can represent slow, timed, and rapid strings without interpreting manually entered observations as physical shot-timing evidence.
- Existing **Qualification Executor** can consume a versioned NRA MQP Conventional Bullseye Pistol profile for practice/simulation qualification only. Official NRA recognition remains separately authority-gated.
- Universal SEC can preserve the exact Champion SKU identity, exact NRA target-specification identity, exact course/profile version, result grade, and claim grade.

No target-ID/SKU production branch is justified. Registration data should bind `publisherProductId/SKU → nraTargetSpecificationId → authorized missionFamily/profileVersion`.

## Plain answers

1. **Do we publicly know what each piece means?** Yes for GB-16/B-16 and GB-3 through direct face-to-NRA identity plus NRA rules. Substantially yes for GB-2, but its exact Champion face/seal mapping is only visible as a low-resolution catalog thumbnail, so it remains Grade B.
2. **Can SCZN3 obtain scoring/course authority without asking Champion or NRA?** Yes for governed practice/simulation against B-16, B-2, and B-3. No additional inquiry is needed for ring scoring, competition stages, or the published MQP course. Current Champion sanctioned-target eligibility and official qualification/recognition claims remain separate gaps.
3. **Same governed spec from multiple printers?** Yes. The NRA specification is the authority; each printer's SKU/packaging is a commercial implementation.
4. **Formal Champion/NRA relationship?** Historical authorized printing is strongly evidenced. Current licensed status is not publicly established and the current NRA directory excludes Champion.
5. **What must be requested if stronger claims are desired?** For GB-2: a first-party face/spec sheet confirming NRA B-2 and its official seal. For current sanctioned use: written current NRA license/authorization status for Champion and the exact eligible SKUs/artwork revisions. For official MQP recognition: the separately governed administration, witness/membership, record, award, and reporting requirements applicable to the requested claim.
6. **Which existing capabilities consume this?** Smart Evidence Capture, precision/open scoring, Cue/Drill primitives, and—only with the exact current MQP profile and claim-grade controls—the Qualification Executor. No target-specific implementation is required.
