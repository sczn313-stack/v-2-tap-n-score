# P-OS — Smart Target Per-Piece Pricing Study

Observed: 2026-08-22  
Model: `POS-SMART-TARGET-PER-PIECE-PRICING-2026-08-22-V1`

## Founder answer

### Ten-cent hypothesis: **REQUIRES TIERING**

**$0.10 is attractive and commercially modest for many normal retail, large,
reactive, and specialty targets. It is economically inappropriate as a uniform
fee across the complete corpus.**

Examples establish both sides:

- A $1.25 single target becomes $1.35: **+$0.10 / +8.0%**.
- A GunFun target priced at $1.06 becomes $1.16: **+9.4%**.
- A Birchwood five-pack at $14.99 becomes $15.49: **+3.3%**.
- A Champion 100-pack at $20.95 becomes $30.95: **+47.7%**.
- An American Target 1,000-pack at $32.50 becomes $132.50:
  **+$100 / +307.7%**.
- A Splatterburst 510-piece micro-target roll at $24.99 becomes $75.99:
  **+$51 / +204.1%**.

The checkout answer is therefore clear:

> On a normal single or premium target, the shooter sees a small dime-sized
> increase for a materially richer product relationship. On inexpensive bulk
> paper or micro-target packages, repeating the dime on every piece overwhelms
> the physical product price and makes the package commercially irrational.

The fee is for the **Smart Target platform relationship**—authorized guidance,
results, SEC, preservation, and publisher relationship where applicable. It is
not payment for printing a QR image.

## Recommended simplest workable structure

Use a two-part, price-guardrailed model:

> **Per enabled target: the lesser of $0.10 or 10% of the publisher's auditable
> net selling price per physical piece.**

> **Plus an annual platform minimum, fully credited against per-piece usage
> fees, set only after SCZN3 measures publisher onboarding/support cost.**

Why this is the smallest correct commercial model:

1. The normal-market message remains simple: **ten cents per enabled Smart
   Target**.
2. The 10% guardrail prevents a three-cent target from carrying a ten-cent
   platform fee.
3. It follows actual publisher economics instead of guessing that annual unit
   volume is a proxy for price.
4. A credited minimum protects the platform from relationships whose actual
   volume never covers onboarding, reporting, support, and governance cost.
5. No annual-minimum dollar amount is supported yet; inventing one would be
   false precision. It should be derived from measured cost and may be waived
   explicitly for a launch partner.

At the guardrail, a $0.0325 bulk target carries a $0.00325 fee, a $0.50 target
carries $0.05, and any target at $1.00 or more reaches the $0.10 ceiling.

## Economics by target class

| Target class | Observed economics | Flat 10¢ assessment | Likely commercial treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inexpensive bulk paper / repair centers | Roughly $0.03–$0.25 per piece in high-count packages | 40% to more than 300% on many products | Must use percentage guardrail or much lower contracted fee. Usually cannot be absorbed. |
| Standard paper | Commonly about $0.25–$0.75 per piece | About 13%–40% | Mix of partial pass-through and negotiated guardrail. |
| Large-format paper/cardboard | Commonly about $0.50–$3+ per piece | About 3%–20% | Often absorbable or easily passed through. |
| Reactive/splatter standard and large | Commonly about $0.50–$3+ per piece | About 3%–20% | Generally attractive; tiny reactive dots/rolls still require guardrail. |
| Specialty, game, diagnostic, qualification | Price depends more on size/material than digital authority | Usually modest on single/large products; can still be excessive in bulk | Do not charge more merely because authority permits a richer capability unless a separate service is sold. |
| One physical sheet with many printed zones | One fee unit | Fee must not multiply by printed bullseyes/regions | Count the sheet, not artwork components. |

## Alternative pricing structures

| Structure | Advantage | Failure mode | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flat per-piece | Simplest to explain, meter, forecast, and invoice | Overwhelms cheap bulk targets; underprices or ignores very high-value relationships | Reject as universal; retain 10¢ as ceiling/headline. |
| Percentage of selling price | Automatically respects product economics | Requires price reporting, net-price definitions, discount/return handling; charges more for premium physical material even if platform cost is identical | Useful as guardrail, not the entire value model. |
| Volume-tiered per-piece | Rewards scale and is familiar commercially | Volume does not solve price heterogeneity: a million $1.25 targets and a million $0.0325 targets cannot sensibly carry the same tier | Insufficient alone. |
| Annual minimum + reduced per-piece | Covers onboarding/support and supports low marginal rates | Can discourage small publishers; minimum is arbitrary until costs are measured | Use only as a fully credited minimum based on measured relationship cost. |
| **Recommended hybrid** | 10¢ ceiling + 10% net-price guardrail + credited minimum | Requires quarterly price-class reporting and a defined net-price policy | **Simplest model that survives the observed corpus.** |

## Absorb versus pass through

Public list price does not reveal printer gross margin, retailer margin,
distributor margin, or contract price. Therefore P-OS cannot truthfully say a
publisher “can absorb” 10¢ merely because the retail percentage looks small.
The evidence supports these narrower conclusions:

- **Most comfortable:** individual/large products at $1+ and reactive/specialty
  products with strong retail value. Ten cents is generally under 10% at $1+.
- **Could absorb or pass through modestly:** products around $0.50–$1.00, where
  10¢ is 10%–20%. Margin evidence is required to distinguish absorption from
  pass-through.
- **Likely requires partial/full pass-through or guardrail:** standard paper
  around $0.25–$0.50.
- **Flat fee inappropriate:** high-count commodity paper and tiny adhesive
  targets below roughly $0.25 per piece, especially the 3–5¢ observations.

No product should receive a higher per-piece fee merely because SCZN3 can
deliver richer governed capabilities. That would couple platform price to an
authority claim that may be optional, unavailable, or separately governed.

## Final commercial statement

The Founder's intuition is directionally correct:

> A $1.25 target becoming $1.35 can look extraordinarily inexpensive to the
> shooter when the additional dime establishes the complete Smart Target
> relationship.

But the complete corpus changes the universal answer:

> **Ten cents is a strong ceiling and a clean normal-target price. It is not a
> universal physical-piece fee. Use a 10% net-price guardrail and a credited
> platform minimum so inexpensive paper can participate without destroying its
> economics.**

This produces the required verdict: **REQUIRES TIERING**, with 10¢ retained as
the commercially attractive headline rather than discarded.
