# P-OS — 11-Printer Annual Target-Volume Investigation

Observed: 2026-08-22  
Model: `POS-11P-ANNUAL-PAPER-TARGET-VOLUME-2026-08-22-V1`

## Founder summary

**Verdict on approximately 30 million individual targets annually: PLAUSIBLE
BUT UNPROVEN.**

The evidence-led planning estimate for the eleven current P-OS publishers is:

| Annual individual paper/cardboard targets | Low | Base | High |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Exact model | 12,050,000 | 24,550,000 | 45,000,000 |
| Rounded planning expression | 12M | 25M | 45M |

Thirty million is 5.45 million pieces, or 22.2%, above the independent base
case. It sits comfortably inside the modeled interval. Public evidence does not
establish audited unit sales or paper-target revenue for any of the eleven,
however, so it would be incorrect to call 30 million a measured fact.

The number is also not facially inconsistent with the size of U.S. shooting.
NSSF reports more than 52.7 million sport/target-shooting participants and an
older industry study estimated 410 million shooting days annually. Thirty
million pieces would equal about 0.57 pieces per participant or one piece per
13.7 shooting days across these eleven publishers. This is only a scale check:
those participation denominators include shooting that uses steel, clay,
homemade/free targets, other publishers, or no fresh paper target.

The three printers contributing the greatest numeric uncertainty are American
Target Company, Birchwood Casey, and EZ Aim / Allen Company. Obtaining their
actual annual paper/cardboard unit sell-through or target-category net revenue
would narrow the combined interval far more than adding another catalog.

## What was counted

The estimated unit is **one physical paper/cardboard target sheet or one
separately marketed shootable target piece sold**.

- Packages are converted into pieces only when package count is supported.
- A sheet printed with multiple bullseyes is one physical sheet, not multiple
  targets.
- Adhesive targets marketed as individual shootable targets are pieces.
- Repair pasters are excluded, even when included in a target package.
- Steel, clay, stands, carriers, hardware, and other accessories are excluded.
- The estimate covers the eleven publishers represented in the current P-OS
  corpus. It is not a total U.S. paper-target market estimate.
- Intended geography is each publisher's total annual volume where evidence
  permits. Sources are heavily U.S.-weighted; unobserved exports remain an
  uncertainty and are not added through a separate unsupported multiplier.

This annual-volume denominator is separate from the existing P-OS ontology:

> Governed Target Specification ≠ Publisher Target Implementation Identity ≠
> Commercial Target Offering ≠ Annual Physical Pieces Sold

Catalog offerings establish what can be bought. They do not establish how many
units were sold.

## Calculation method

No single method was forced across every publisher because the available
evidence differs materially. The model uses three bounded approaches:

1. **Revenue conversion** — for National, U.S. Target, and Allen, public total
   revenue indicators are combined with official package prices, a broad
   target-category share, and an assumed manufacturer realization per piece.
   The unobserved shares are why the intervals remain wide.
2. **Channel/package conversion** — for Splatterburst, Birchwood Casey, and
   Champion, official package counts and channel breadth are combined with
   selected marketplace-velocity indicators. Marketplace models are discounted
   and are never represented as audited sales.
3. **Capacity/catalog/institutional bounding** — for Baker, GunFun, Highwild,
   American Target, and Speedwell, official bulk packaging, catalog material
   mix, printing/manufacturing evidence, range coverage, and institutional
   channels bound plausible order-of-magnitude volume. Catalog breadth alone is
   never converted mechanically into units.

The low case is not a known floor, and the high case is not a capacity ceiling.
They are evidence-consistent planning bounds. The base is the current most
defensible point estimate inside those bounds.

## Why 30 million is plausible but unproven

### Evidence supporting plausibility

- The eleven include several national mass-retail brands, specialist
  range-retail suppliers, and institutional/government printers rather than
  eleven small direct-only catalogs.
- Paper targets are consumable; ranges and programs buy bulk cases of 100, 250,
  500, 600, 1,000, and sometimes more than 5,000 pieces.
- Selected marketplace models show meaningful monthly package velocity for
  individual SKUs before direct, distributor, institutional, and other retail
  channels are considered.
- The independent base is 24.55M, so 30M requires a 22.2% upward movement, not
  an order-of-magnitude change.
- External participation scale is large enough that 30M does not imply an
  implausible number of targets per shooter or range day.

### Evidence preventing a “YES” finding

- None of the eleven publishes audited annual paper/cardboard unit sales.
- Enterprise revenue for GSM/Birchwood and Allen is not target-category revenue.
- American Target and Speedwell, two potentially high-volume institutional
  printers, disclose no current throughput or sales denominator.
- Marketplace analytics cover selected listings and can omit channels, count
  estimated rather than actual orders, or overlap with other channel evidence.
- Package mix strongly affects pieces: one transaction can represent one sheet,
  ten sheets, 100 sheets, or 1,000 sheets.
- Public sources do not reliably distinguish manufacturer sell-in, retailer
  sell-through, and ultimate shooter consumption.

Therefore, **30M is suitable as a labeled scenario inside a 12M–45M planning
range. It is not suitable as an established market fact.** Until higher-grade
evidence arrives, P-OS should use **24.55M as the base model** and retain all
three cases in business planning.

same piece definition would improve all eleven estimates rapidly.

## Recommended evidence request

For each publisher, request the following without asking for customer-specific
confidential data:

1. Annual paper/cardboard target sheets shipped in the last complete year.
2. Annual packages shipped and package-weighted mean pieces per package.
3. Paper/cardboard target net sales, excluding steel and accessories.
4. Direct / distributor / retail / marketplace / institutional percentage mix.
5. Whether figures are sell-in, sell-through, shipments, or recognized revenue.
6. Returns/cancellations treatment and geographic scope.

Those six answers would replace most of the current assumptions and allow P-OS
to publish a substantially narrower revision rather than rebuild the work.
