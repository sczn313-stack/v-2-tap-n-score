# SCZN3 SHOOTING KNOWLEDGE — FIRST SYNTHESIS

**Corpus:** 12 preserved publisher/printer bodies of evidence  
**Synthesis date:** 2026-08-23  
**Knowledge-philosophy revision:** 2, Founder-directed  
**Status:** living knowledge synthesis; not implementation or production activation

## Founder answer

SCZN3 now knows enough to form a useful first model of shooter improvement, but
its strength is uneven.

> **Shooter improvement is a learning loop: define the shooter's objective,
> observe repeatable evidence, apply the best relevant knowledge SCZN3 has
> synthesized, make one useful change, compare the result, and learn from what
> happens next.**

The knowledge-building loop is:

> **MINE → COMMONIZE → RECONCILE → SYNTHESIZE → DELIVER → LEARN**

The delivery standard is:

> **SCZN3 always tells the truth to its audience.**

The twelve publishers are inputs to learning, not twelve competing knowledge
authorities and not twelve output folders. **There is no governing-authority
hierarchy for general shooting knowledge.** SCZN3 learns the concepts, relationships,
conditions, limits, and useful next actions that survive reconciliation across
the evidence, then expresses that accumulated understanding originally and
neutrally.

Sources remain underneath the knowledge product for validation, integrity,
licensing, historical correction, and specialized rule provenance. They do not
determine the organization of the knowledge base. **Source recognition is not
the product. The accumulated knowledge is the product.**

This does not erase the separate requirements for licensed source expression,
current competition/course rules, official qualification claims, or other
specialized institutional acts. Those constrain particular uses and claims;
they do not prevent SCZN3 from learning and synthesizing general knowledge.

Across the corpus, the recurring useful functions of a target are:

1. make the intended aim or response unambiguous;
2. make impacts visible enough to evaluate;
3. make displacement, dispersion, score, sequence, or state measurable;
4. supply a defined drill, diagnostic, course, game, or progression;
5. preserve comparable evidence so the next attempt has context.

The strongest present knowledge concerns **visible feedback, zeroing geometry,
target/course identity, formal scoring structures, pistol diagnostic content,
and rule-driven drills**. The corpus is only partial on **general fundamentals,
recoil management, advancement criteria, and contextual correction**. It is
thin on **shotgun instruction, movement, positional rifle work, equipment fit,
human coaching, and safety doctrine**.

This result is not “everything publishers say.” Repeated package variants,
marketing synonyms, artwork, and the same standardized face printed by multiple
manufacturers were collapsed. The synthesis preserves the difference between:

- an observed result;
- a publisher-authored label or instruction;
- a governing-organization rule;
- a cross-source P-OS synthesis; and
- a synthesized conclusion that remains uncertain or needs stronger evidence.

## How the corpus was read

The pass covered Baker Targets, GunFun Shooting Targets, Splatterburst,
Highwild, Birchwood Casey, EZ Aim / Allen Company, Champion Targets, American
Target Company, National Target Company, Speedwell, U.S. Target, and Thompson.

The unit of analysis was a **knowledge-bearing lineage**, not a SKU. Sources
included normalized product descriptions, printed rules and guides already in
the corpus, target/profile authority, NRA specification mappings, course and
capability analyses, and the Thompson pistol-training deep dive. Catalog-only
records with no instructional meaning were reviewed as context but did not
become teaching claims.

Knowledge method:

> **MINE → COMMONIZE → RECONCILE → SYNTHESIZE → DELIVER → LEARN**

- **Mine:** extract the usable concepts, observations, problems, methods,
  conditions, measurements, and next actions.
- **Commonize:** collapse different expressions of substantially the same idea
  into one structured understanding.
- **Reconcile:** merge equivalent ideas; split apparently conflicting ideas
  when distance, firearm, handedness, goal, or evidence grade makes them
  context-dependent.
- **Synthesize:** state only the smallest useful shooter principle supported by
  the combined evidence.
- **Deliver:** express the knowledge in original, audience-appropriate language
  that tells the truth about certainty and limits.
- **Learn:** compare future evidence with the current model, strengthen or
  qualify it, and preserve the change history.

## WHAT SCZN3 NOW KNOWS

SCZN3 now has a coherent first understanding of how target-based shooting
practice can support improvement:

- begin with the shooter's objective, not the target catalog;
- make the task, aim, response, and success condition clear;
- collect enough repeatable evidence to distinguish an event from a pattern;
- separate location, dispersion, timing, score, and sequence before combining
  them into a conclusion;
- compare like with like—same meaningful setup variables, clearly identified;
- change one useful variable and confirm with new evidence;
- use drills, diagnostics, courses, games, and progression as different
  learning structures rather than treating every printed target alike;
- preserve uncertainty when the evidence supports more than one explanation;
- preserve prior results so improvement is learned over time rather than
  guessed from memory.

The following domain synthesis explains that knowledge in detail.

## The first synthesized knowledge model

### 1. Fundamentals — PARTIAL

The Thompson material gives the clearest explicit fundamentals set: stable
body position, consistent grip, breath control, sight alignment, deliberate
trigger squeeze, and follow-through. Diagnostic products from Thompson and
GunFun reinforce that the value of these fundamentals is repeatability: the
same setup and operation should produce a coherent group rather than unrelated
impacts.

What SCZN3 can learn: teach fundamentals as a **repeatable pre-shot and
post-shot process**, then evaluate a group—not as isolated slogans and not from
one unexplained hole.

Limit: the corpus does not establish one universal stance, grip, breathing
cycle, or trigger technique across every firearm and shooting context.

### 2. Accuracy and grouping — STRONG

A single impact establishes location. A group begins to establish
repeatability, center, dispersion, bias, and outliers. Multiple clean aim areas
allow deliberate repetitions without old impacts contaminating the next
comparison. Grids and clean-cut or high-visibility impacts make group
evaluation easier.

The useful sequence supported across zeroing, diagnostic, multi-bull, and
record-keeping targets is:

> **Declare the aim → fire a repeatable group → locate group center and spread →
> compare with the intended point → change one variable → confirm with a new
> group.**

The corpus does not support treating every outlier as an equipment correction
or treating group center as a diagnosis of shooter cause.

### 3. Sight and optic use — PARTIAL TO STRONG

Sight-in grids, high-contrast aiming marks, thermal signatures, and reduced
distance target faces all exist to make the sighting relationship observable.
Known dimensions can translate target displacement into a correction only when
distance, target scale, sight/optic units, and correction direction are known.

Reduced targets also teach an important limit: a target may simulate the
angular presentation of another distance, but that does not silently reproduce
every ballistic, wind, recoil, or environmental condition of firing at the
full distance.

### 4. Trigger operation — PARTIAL

Thompson explicitly associates several printed group patterns with trigger
operation labels and corrective text; its fundamentals emphasize trigger
squeeze and follow-through. The corpus therefore supports a cautious teaching
principle: abrupt or inconsistent trigger operation can disturb the sighting
relationship, and the shooter should evaluate it over a group.

It does **not** support declaring “you jerked the trigger” from a single impact.
Any diagnostic conclusion must remain conditional on enough evidence,
handedness where relevant, context, and an honest ambiguity rule. When a
specific teaching is presented from a publisher program, attribution and
licensing remain preserved underneath delivery.

### 5. Grip and control — PARTIAL

The diagnostic corpus identifies thumb pressure, heel pressure, over-gripping,
wrist break, and related grip/control patterns. The reconciled knowledge is
not that one chart universally diagnoses the hand. It is that grip and wrist
consistency can change where a group forms, and handedness or profile-specific
geometry matters.

SCZN3 should never mirror a right-handed chart by assumption or convert a
publisher nickname into a universal causal conclusion.

### 6. Zeroing — STRONG

Zeroing is not “move the reticle toward the hole” as a context-free rule. It is
a measurable relationship among firearm/ammunition, optic or sight, range,
declared aim, group evidence, target geometry, desired point of impact, and
the sight's adjustment units.

The corpus repeatedly supports:

- use a target whose scale and aim points are appropriate to the intended
  distance and sight system;
- use a group rather than chase individual shots;
- measure the group relative to the declared aim;
- compute a correction only from compatible measurements, units, and a valid
  correction model;
- fire a fresh confirmation group after adjustment;
- preserve load and setup facts when comparing later results.

A “sight-in” label establishes intended use; it does not alone supply every
measurement or correction rule.

### 7. Diagnosing shot and group patterns — STRONG CONTENT, PARTIAL METHOD

The corpus is rich in publisher-authored diagnostic language, especially for
pistol shooting. Thompson supplies right/left treatments, named patterns,
fundamentals, and corrective statements. GunFun and other tutor-style targets
confirm this as a recurring product behavior.

The strongest current model is:

> **Evidence pattern → compare with the best relevant diagnostic knowledge →
> present the supported interpretation and useful next action → preserve
> ambiguity or no reliable conclusion when the evidence does not fit.**

The corpus is weak on machine-readable thresholds: minimum shots, centroid vs.
majority logic, dispersion limits, mixed patterns, confidence, and validation
against instructor judgment. A printed diagnostic chart is useful evidence and
may carry a specific teaching program, but it is not proof that its causal model
is universally valid.

### 8. Recoil management — THIN TO PARTIAL

Recoil anticipation, “jumping the gun,” wrist behavior, grip, and follow-through
appear in the diagnostic material. This supports recognizing recoil management
as a real knowledge domain and preserving relevant evidence.

It does not yet support a comprehensive recoil curriculum. The corpus is thin
on return-to-sight behavior, cadence, firearm fit, stance interaction, recoil
impulse across platforms, and validated corrective progression.

### 9. Speed versus accuracy — PARTIAL

TAC's Time Factor, timed/rapid-fire target specifications, time-plus-penalty
systems, and timed practice targets show that speed and accuracy can be joined
only by an explicit rule. A clock without a defined response standard is not a
complete result; a score without reliable timing is not a timed result.

The synthesized principle is:

> **First define what counts as an acceptable response, then measure time over
> the exact interval, and preserve accuracy and time separately before applying
> any explicit composite.**

The corpus does not justify one universal “go faster” threshold.

### 10. Drills and repetition — STRONG

The clearest drills define some combination of start condition, cue, intended
response, shot count, sequence, distance, timing policy, and completion state.
TAC is the strongest example: low-ready start, a partner/caller, exact cue sets,
recommended repetitions, pistol/rifle distances, zone values, and an admitted
time interval.

Repeated aim points and replacement centers also support clean repetition, but
they do not create a drill by themselves. A selectable array of colors, shapes,
numbers, or silhouettes is drill **potential** until a versioned rule says what
to present and how to judge the response.

### 11. Progression — PARTIAL

The corpus distinguishes three ideas that must not be merged:

1. **ordinal sequence:** what target or exercise comes next;
2. **readiness:** what evidence proves the shooter should advance;
3. **longitudinal improvement:** what changed across preserved sessions.

Thompson authorizes a three-stage order—Group-Shooter, Trouble-Shooter, TAC—but
does not publish readiness thresholds. Baker's Bullseye Challenge establishes
progression intent, but its authoritative guide is still required. Therefore a
publisher can establish “next” without establishing “ready.”

### 12. Pistol knowledge — STRONGEST FIREARM-SPECIFIC AREA

The corpus contains slow, timed, and rapid-fire faces; many qualification and
competition forms; handgun diagnostic systems; and publisher-authored
progression and cue drills. It supports strong work on group evaluation,
trigger/grip pattern guidance, short-range cue response, formal scoring, and
practice-course execution.

Its major gaps are movement, drawing, reloads, malfunction work, equipment fit,
and independently validated coaching logic. Those behaviors should not be
inferred from silhouette artwork or product marketing.

### 13. Rifle knowledge — PARTIAL TO STRONG

Rifle knowledge is strongest around sight-in, zeroing, distance-specific and
reduced faces, formal prone/position target specifications, load records, and
qualification/course structures. NRA and military-style mappings demonstrate
that distance, position, slow/rapid phase, and exact face are properties of a
defined course—not interchangeable artwork.

The corpus is thinner on natural-position coaching, sling use, positional
transitions, wind reading, external ballistics, and field shooting.

### 14. Shotgun knowledge — THIN

The corpus contains shotgun-compatible products, turkey imagery, shotgun
qualification references, and some patterning/intended-use signals. That is
not yet a complete shotgun knowledge base.

It lacks an adequately supported synthesis for pattern percentage and density,
point of aim versus point of impact, choke/load comparison, acceptable pattern
criteria by purpose, moving targets, leads, mounting, or fit. “Works with a
shotgun” is compatibility—not instruction.

### 15. Competition preparation — PARTIAL TO STRONG

The corpus can strongly establish exact target identity, scoring regions,
course stages, shot allocation, penalties, timing, and preserved practice
results when the current relevant rules are known. Multiple publishers
printing one NRA face reinforces that the standardized specification is distinct
from printer identity and package SKU.

Practice on the correct face can familiarize a shooter with visible geometry
and scoring. It does not by itself reproduce match administration, commands,
equipment rules, range conditions, or current governing rules.

### 16. Qualification preparation — STRONG STRUCTURAL KNOWLEDGE,
### UNEVEN COURSE EVIDENCE

A qualification target is not a qualification. The course may add stages,
distance, timing, commands, round counts, scoring, pass threshold, weapon
conditions, and administrative requirements.

The useful three-grade model is:

- **evidence-only:** preserve what occurred;
- **practice/simulation:** evaluate against a complete, current course
  definition without claiming an official credential;
- **official:** only when administration, witness/instructor, facility,
  identity, record, reporting, and credential authorities are also satisfied.

This is one of the strongest lessons learned and prevents a paper target or
printer label from being mistaken for completion of an official program.

### 17. Beginner knowledge — PARTIAL

Beginner-oriented evidence favors clear high-contrast aim points, visible
impacts, uncomplicated tasks, multiple clean repetitions, and immediate review.
Large or reactive marks can reduce the burden of seeing impacts; smaller aim
points can be introduced as precision demands increase.

This is not a universal “large target first” rule. Target size, distance,
firearm, vision, and the learning objective determine what is appropriate. The
corpus also lacks a comprehensive beginner safety and equipment-fit curriculum.

### 18. Common problems and corrective actions — PARTIAL

The corpus can organize common evidence patterns around:

- broad or inconsistent groups;
- a coherent group displaced from aim;
- trigger/grip/wrist-associated publisher patterns;
- recoil anticipation/follow-through concerns;
- correct response delivered late, early, or out of sequence;
- incomplete course evidence;
- equipment/zero mismatch.

The correct response is not always “correct the shooter.” First distinguish
measurement failure, task mismatch, insufficient evidence, equipment state,
and repeatability. Advice should match the firearm, shooter objective,
conditions, and strength of the underlying knowledge—and it should permit “no
reliable conclusion.”

## Additional knowledge the corpus revealed

### Visibility is an evidence capability, not a coaching conclusion

Reactive splatter, contrasting colors, thermal marks, steel movement/sound,
and clean-hole paper reduce the delay between shot and observation. They can
make groups and corrections easier to see. They do not explain why an impact
occurred or make a target's scoring/tactical meaning authoritative.

### Target design manages cognitive load

Multiple bullseyes isolate repetitions; grids aid measurement; replacement
centers preserve a clean reference; cue arrays enable selection and memory;
scenario panels enable discrimination; games sustain attention. These are
different teaching affordances even when all can preserve shot evidence.

### A course is a learning structure, not a sheet of paper

The same physical face can support evidence, practice, competition,
qualification, diagnostic, or recreational learning. The intended activity
determines what observations matter and what conclusion can truthfully follow.
The paper alone does not choose that activity for the shooter.

### Historical evidence is part of learning

Champion record-keeping targets, Baker progression, Thompson's published
sequence, and SCZN3's preserved SEC model converge on the value of comparison.
Improvement claims need comparable setup facts and unaltered historical
results—not memory or a reinterpreted old score.

### Safety and legal/tactical doctrine are conspicuous gaps

Product copy sometimes uses “self-defense,” “law enforcement,” “threat,” or
anatomical imagery. Those words and images do not establish safety doctrine,
legal judgment, tactical correctness, or a complete use-of-force knowledge
base. The first synthesis deliberately does not manufacture those subjects
from marketing.

## WHAT BECAME STRONGER THROUGH COMMONIZATION

Commonization produced knowledge that is more useful than any repeated catalog
description:

- dozens of sight-in products became one coherent **zeroing and confirmation
  model** instead of dozens of “easy sight-in” claims;
- reactive paper, thermal marks, clean-hole stock, contrasting color, and steel
  feedback became one **visibility-as-evidence** concept;
- multi-bull, replacement-center, and repeated-aim products became one model
  for **isolating repetitions and preserving clean comparisons**;
- diagnostic charts became a shared model of **pattern evidence, context,
  ambiguity, and useful next action**, while their universal causal validity
  remains unproven;
- slow, timed, rapid, time-factor, and penalty products became one model for
  **keeping accuracy and time separate until an explicit method combines them**;
- competition and qualification faces became a shared understanding that a
  **target face, a course, a practice result, and an official outcome are
  different things**;
- bundles, printed sequences, challenges, and preserved history became a model
  that separates **what comes next, readiness to advance, and improvement over
  time**;
- games, cue targets, and scenario products became a common model of
  **attention, selection, sequence, state, and feedback** rather than a list of
  themed artwork.

## WHAT CONFLICTS WERE RECONCILED

| Apparent conflict | Reconciliation |
|---|---|
| Large/high-visibility aim points versus small precision aim points | Different goals and distances. Visibility reduces observation burden; smaller references may test precision. Neither is universally superior. |
| One shot versus a group | One shot records an event; a group provides stronger evidence of repeatability, bias, and dispersion. Some rule systems still score single events. |
| Shoot slowly versus add time | Accuracy-only and timed work answer different questions. Timing is added only after the accepted response and interval are defined. |
| Diagnostic chart says “cause X” versus ambiguous evidence | Treat the chart as one teaching input; require enough evidence, relevant context and handedness, and permit unmatched/ambiguous outcomes. Do not universalize it. |
| Reduced face versus full-distance experience | A reduced face can preserve specified angular geometry; it does not recreate all full-distance ballistics or conditions. |
| Reactive feedback versus better skill | Visibility improves observation. Improvement still requires an interpreted learning loop and comparable repetition. |
| Qualification label versus official qualification | A target identifies the face; the governing course and administrator establish the qualification claim. |
| Game/scenario artwork versus decision meaning | Artwork may create selectable state, but the intended exercise and evaluation method must be explicit; artwork does not establish legal or tactical judgment. |

## WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAIN

The following subjects are useful enough to retain but not strong enough for a
universal conclusion:

- general fundamentals across contexts;
- trigger, grip, wrist, anticipation, and follow-through guidance;
- speed/accuracy development;
- pistol progression criteria;
- rifle positional skill and load development;
- beginner sequencing;
- competition preparation beyond target/course execution;
- correction selection when shooter and equipment causes overlap.

## WHAT KNOWLEDGE IS STILL MISSING

- comprehensive shotgun fundamentals and pattern interpretation;
- movement, transitions, drawing, reloads, malfunction work, and use of cover;
- wind, field conditions, external ballistics, and positional rifle coaching;
- firearm fit, vision, accessibility, and adaptive shooting;
- validated minimum-evidence and confidence rules for group diagnosis;
- general readiness/advancement standards;
- comprehensive, reliable safety curriculum;
- legal, tactical, medical, and use-of-force doctrine;
- independent effectiveness evidence showing which printed guidance improves
  which shooters under which conditions.

## WHAT WE SHOULD DELIBERATELY LEARN NEXT

The next research should be knowledge-gap driven, not publisher-count driven.

1. **Shotgun knowledge package:** reliable patterning, choke/load
   comparison, point-of-impact, fit, moving-target fundamentals, and accepted
   pattern criteria by purpose.
2. **Diagnostic validation package:** obtain publisher machine-readable match
   rules, then compare them with qualified-instructor and empirical evidence;
   define minimum shots, ambiguity, confidence, and mixed-pattern handling.
3. **Rifle fundamentals package:** position, natural point of aim, sling,
   breathing, follow-through, wind, and transition from reduced to full range.
4. **Progression/readiness package:** secure Baker's complete Bullseye Challenge
   guide and Thompson advancement criteria; study how readiness differs from
   mere sequence.
5. **Beginner learning model:** safety, setup, first groups, feedback,
   correction limits, and when human instruction is required.
6. **Speed-development evidence:** how accuracy standards, par times, response
   windows, and difficulty should progress without encouraging speed beyond the
   shooter's control and objective.
7. **Movement/manipulation package:** reliable dry/live-fire boundaries,
   draw, reload, transitions, movement, and malfunction work—none should be
   inferred from current target art.
8. **Accessibility package:** vision, color contrast, handedness, mobility,
   hearing, language, and adaptive equipment.
9. **Effectiveness studies:** distinguish popular target advice from guidance
   supported by measured shooter improvement.

For every new input, SCZN3 should ask:

> **Does this strengthen an existing claim, qualify it, contradict it under the
> same conditions, or expose an actually new knowledge domain?**

## Knowledge product boundary

This is a durable, living knowledge map. It may inform future research,
specifications, shooter-facing language, publisher collaboration, and product
design. Knowledge is not limited to what SCZN3 can implement today.

It does not by itself:

- establish that every synthesized idea is universally true;
- license source text, images, rules, or voice content;
- make a product executable;
- establish official qualification, legal, tactical, medical, or safety
  authority;
- convert marketing copy into fact;
- implement any new capability;
- change production, registration, architecture, or existing target behavior.

## Final synthesis

> **SCZN3 should help a shooter improve by understanding the objective, making
> the evidence observable, applying the best relevant knowledge it has learned,
> proposing one useful next action, comparing the result, and learning again.**

The current knowledge does not support one universal coaching script. It
supports something more useful: SCZN3 can synthesize across many inputs,
deliver an original and context-aware explanation, preserve source integrity
underneath when needed, and let uncertainty remain an honest result.

This is not the end of the report. It is the first version of a living SCZN3
Shooting Knowledge base whose purpose is endless accumulation:

> **MINE → COMMONIZE → RECONCILE → SYNTHESIZE → DELIVER → LEARN**
